# Evolution geology — research notes

The research behind `plans/V2_GEOLOGY_PLAN.md` (2026-10-19): Madeline James's guide read in full, then five research
passes on what the guide leaves open for a procedural, history-driven model. Numbers marked *derived* are arithmetic on
the cited figures; *contested* marks claims the literature disputes; several figures come from abstracts only.

## 1. The guide (Madeline James, Parts 11–12)

**Part 11 — Rocks and Minerology** (madelinejameswrites.com/blog/minerology). Sources: Perkins, *Mineralogy* 2nd ed.;
reviewed by Nikolai Hersfeldt (Worldbuilding Pasta) and RossBayGeo.
- Rock cycle; igneous by silica (felsic ≥ 65%, intermediate 55–60%, mafic 45–50%, ultramafic ≤ 40%), extrusive vs
  plutonic; plutonic rock exposed in shields and mountain cores; basalt at ridges, hotspots, LIPs; andesite at arcs;
  rhyolite at hotspots, rifts and arcs.
- LIPs (> 100,000 km²): ⅔ at past hotspots and ridges, ⅓ now; mostly basaltic, a few andesitic (arcs) or rhyolitic
  (hotspots); with sills, dykes and contact aureoles; "associated with a large number of important deposits".
- Sedimentary: < 1% of the crust but ~80% of land surface; clastic (mudrock 80%, sandstone 10–20%), organic (coal),
  chemical (carbonate, evaporite); basins: foreland, rift, intracontinental, graben.
- Metamorphic (~12% of land): regional (orogens, shields), contact, hydrothermal, dynamic, impact; Barrovian
  slate → phyllite → schist → gneiss → migmatite; blueschist/eclogite rare at the surface; older ranges show higher grade.
- Archean cratons: shields (exposed) vs platforms (covered); dome-and-keel TTG gneiss between greenstone belts
  (komatiite, basalt; gold, nickel, copper-zinc, BIF).
- **Maps**: (1) orogenies — active, inactive, past (eroded flat); (2) past shallow seas (Cretaceous highstand ~550 ft)
  and continental shelves (narrow on rifted and subduction coasts, wide elsewhere); (3) major rock types: cratons;
  igneous (LIPs, arcs, granitic in orogens, patches in past orogens); metamorphic (shields, orogens, aureoles);
  carbonate (around shields, collision zones, past seas); sandstone (lowlands round mountains, fold belts); sediments
  (basins, shelves); ocean sediment and ridge basalt.

**Part 12 — Deposits and Gemology** (/blog/deposits-and-gemology-extended). Sources: Perkins ch. 9; Hauptmann,
*Archaeometallurgy*; Andrew Jackson's *Ore Deposits 101*; GIA Gems & Geology columns (Palke & Shigley).
- 90% of deposits are hydrothermally concentrated; depth classes: deep crust/mantle, mesothermal (1–10 km), epithermal
  (0–1 km), surface. Only what is at or near the surface matters.
- Deposit catalogue: kimberlites; mafic layered complexes; porphyries (Cu-Mo; Cu-Au in island arcs); skarns; IOCG;
  greenstone gold and BIF; mesothermal veins; pegmatites; epithermal; Carlin; Witwatersrand; VMS; SEDEX; MVT and fahlore;
  stratiform; placers; bog iron; laterites (iron, bauxite, nickel, gold); gossans; supergene enrichment; evaporites;
  oolitic ironstone; BIF.
- "Deposits are a scale from small, unmineable scarce deposits to industrial-scale productivity" (Nikolai) — not
  video-game nodes. Ancient deposits (native copper and silver at the surface) are long gone today.
- Metals known before Rome: copper (8th–5th mill. BCE), gold (5th), lead (5th–3rd), silver (4th–3rd), tin (3rd),
  iron (3rd–2nd), antimony, mercury, zinc (100–30 BCE).
- **One map per technological age**: Stone (flint, obsidian, quartzite, nephrite, agate, basalt, rhyolite); Fuel (peat,
  coal on the last supercontinent's 45°N–45°S, wood); Copper (native copper from fahlore and supergene zones, arsenical
  copper, placer/laterite/gossan gold, silver); Bronze (copper: VMS > SEDEX > skarn > few porphyry/IOCG; tin belts in
  former continental margins and their placers; arsenic; bronze potential = copper + tin or arsenic + fuel; gold; silver
  from lead; alum); Iron (bog iron, skarn, laterite, oolitic, meteoritic iron; charcoal; zinc and brass); Industrial
  (greenstone gold, porphyry copper, IOCG then BIF iron). Building stone (limestone, sandstone, granite, marble, clay).
- Mapping principle: grade × size → poor / moderate / rich regions; the richer wins where they overlap.
- Gems: metamorphic (jade, tanzanite, lapis), magmatic (kimberlite diamonds, pegmatite tourmaline/aquamarine/emerald/
  topaz, corundum, peridot, amethyst, garnet), sedimentary (turquoise, agate, malachite, opal), placer gems.

## 2. Metallogeny, preservation, density

- **Porphyry Cu-Mo-Au**: arcs; contraction, thickening and fast uplift favour big, high-grade ones; ridge, seamount and
  flat-slab subduction help; strongly extensional arcs barren; post-collisional ones Au-rich (Sillitoe 2010:
  pyrite.test.utah.edu/fieldtrips/SEGFperu-2012/Readings/Sillitoe%202010.pdf). Form 1–6 km deep (mean ~2); epochs 10–20
  Myr. ~¾ of world copper. Grade–tonnage lognormal: tonnage 33 / 240 / 1500 Mt (90/50/10%), Cu 0.24 / 0.44 / 0.75%
  (USGS OFR 2008-1155: pubs.usgs.gov/of/2008/1155/).
- **Preservation** (Wilkinson & Kesler 2007, abstract): exposed ages are lognormal with modes set by formation depth —
  epithermal (~0.5 km) ~3 Ma, porphyry (~1.9 km) ~11 Ma, orogenic gold (~10 km) ~199 Ma; ~62% of Phanerozoic porphyries
  already eroded (*derived* half-life ~160 Myr); ~1% of surviving ones exposed. Wet regions: porphyries young and sparse;
  dry: old and abundant (geomorphology.earth.indiana.edu/news-events/_news/2015-yanites-kesler.html).
- **Exhumation rates** (km/Myr): active belts 0.1–1, central Andes ≤ 0.25, Papua > 10 (esurf.copernicus.org/articles/13/
  683/2025/); river basins mean 0.218, median 0.054; bare outcrop 0.012 (GSA Today 21/8). Exposure window (*derived*):
  buried while erosion < top of the orebody, exposed while between top and bottom, gone beyond.
- **Orogenic gold**: accretionary and collisional belts under compression, late, near crustal-scale faults; 2–20 km;
  none in belts < ~50 Ma (pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70023338; /70023618).
- **VMS**: back-arc, fore-arc and ridge rifts; all ages, peaks at supercontinent assembly; back-arc ones survive best
  (ecite.utas.edu.au/67821). **MVT**: foreland and forebulge platform carbonates, brines driven by the rising range, tens
  of Myr after the host. **SEDEX**: intracontinental and failed rifts, rifted margins; post-2 Ga (pubs.usgs.gov/
  publication/70146125).
- **Tin**: reduced, S-type crustal-melt granites in collision zones (Cornubian 295–275 Ma) and inboard arcs; Andean
  zoning trench → inboard: Fe/IOCG → Cu-Mo-Au → Cu-Pb-Zn-Ag → Sn-W-Bi-Ag (~900 km tin belt; Sillitoe: above ~300 km slab
  depth, *derived* ~500 km inboard) (redalyc.org/journal/1739/173956934007; app.ingemmet.gob.pe/biblioteca/pdf/P-519.pdf).
- **Archean-only**: greenstone gold, BIF (3.8–1.8 Ga, peak ~2.5; again 0.8–0.6 Ga with Snowball glaciations), Wits-type
  gold (one on Earth, ~⅓–40% of all gold mined), layered PGE, most IOCG — the start world inherits them.
- **Mineral systems** (Wyborn et al. 1994; McCuaig & Hronsky): source, pathway, trap, preservation — any one missing →
  low prospectivity; timing matters, not just coincidence (dmp.wa.gov.au/msa/mineral-systems-816.aspx). Fuzzy operators:
  AND = min, OR = max, product, sum, gamma = sum^γ·product^(1−γ); intermediate γ best; logistic memberships beat classes.
  *Plan*: within an ingredient a high-γ sum, across ingredients a weighted geometric mean.
- **Densities** (per 100,000 km² of permissive ground unless noted): porphyry Cu 2–128, median 15
  (pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70197200); VMS 0.001 / 0.007 / 0.037 per km²; placer gold ~0.006 per km²; kimberlite
  0.000012 per km² (pubs.usgs.gov/pp/p1640a). Deposits within 2 km are one. *Derived*: smaller than a cell at every
  Detail — store density per km², sample Poisson(ρ·A). Districts 5–30 km, deposits 0.1–1 km apart, ages within a district
  up to ~5 Myr. Sizes lognormal with a Pareto tail; Zipf in mature provinces (*contested* as a predictor).

## 3. Rock types (lithology)

- **GLiM** (Hartmann & Moosdorf 2012; computed here from the 0.5° grid, doi.pangaea.de/10.1594/PANGAEA.788537), share of
  ice-free land: unconsolidated 27.9%, siliciclastic 18.1%, mixed sedimentary 16.0%, carbonate 7.9%, evaporite 0.3%,
  metamorphic 14.7%, plutonic 6.4 / 0.4 / 0.7% (felsic / intermediate / basic), volcanic 3.9 / 1.9 / 1.1% (basic /
  intermediate / felsic) + pyroclastic 0.6%. (*Contested*: newer maps put unconsolidated near half.)
- **Provinces** (Hasterok et al. 2022, github.com/dhasterok/global_tectonics), share of continental crust: orogenic belt
  25.9%, shield 19.9%, volcanic arc 19.5%, craton 11.0%, rifts 7.1%, accretionary complex 5.6%, passive margin 3.9%, LIP
  2.1%. Archean basement ~24%.
- **By setting** (GLiM × Hasterok, %): foredeep 66 loose sediment; intracratonic basin 39 loose + 34 shale + 14
  carbonate; shield 24 metamorphic; arc 13 volcanic, 9 plutonic. Young orogens mostly sedimentary/volcanic at the
  surface; old ones crystalline. Top 5% of land by height: 52% sedimentary, 16% volcanic, 11% plutonic, 9% metamorphic.
  Archean cratons: 41% crystalline exposed, 59% covered — in the tropics as at high latitude (glaciation isn't the only
  cause; *contested*).
- **Arcs**: intrusive:extrusive 2:1 to 10:1; belt 50–200 km wide; more felsic as crust thickens (20–35 km oceanic, up to
  80 km Andean); flat slabs (~10% of subduction) move magmatism inland then shut it off (Zellmer 2019,
  frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2019.00040).
- **Basin fill**: foreland (Ganges > 12 km), intracratonic (Michigan ~4.9 km), rift (Baikal ~7 km), carbonate platform
  (Bahamas ~8 km).
- **Cratons**: Pilbara domes 30–70 km (as small as 10–15) at ~120 km wavelength, thin greenstone keels; Superior linear
  belts (Abitibi ~700 × 200 km); Zimbabwe 81% granitoid, 17% greenstone (greenstone 15–35%, *contested*); mobile belts
  250–500 km wide (Limpopo, Trans-Hudson). At 100 km/px keels are sub-pixel: exaggerate 1.5–2×.
- **Erosion depth → grade** (*derived* working thresholds): < 5 km unmetamorphosed, 5–12 slate/phyllite, 12–20 schist,
  20–30 gneiss/migmatite, > 30 granulite (20–30% shallower in hot crust); volcanic cover gone after 3–5 km, plutons from
  5–15, gneissic arc roots below ~25 (Sierra Nevada). Old orogens erode ~0.02–0.06 km/Myr: grade is set mostly while a
  belt is active. Isostasy: removing h of crust lowers the surface ~0.17 h — track erosion depth apart from height.
- **Epeiric seas**: highstand +100 ± 50 m to 250–350 m (*contested*); ~35% of today's land was flooded at the Cretaceous
  peak — parameterise by flooded *fraction* (20–40%), or better record real past submergence. Warm-water carbonate to
  ~30°; sediment supply suppresses it.
- **Colours**: CGMW age colours (timescalefoundation.org/charts/RGB.pdf); BGR IGME5000 lithology colours — sand
  255/255/179, sandstone 242/217/115, mudstone 179/140/89, limestone 102/179/242, granite 255/77/77, gabbro 242/64/115,
  rhyolite 199/153/242, andesite 145/69/235, basalt 102/0/255, slate 128/242/153, schist 51/166/102, gneiss 97/224/122.
  Desaturate ~30–40%, multiply with the hillshade.

## 4. Climate-linked deposits

- **Bauxite** (Price et al. 1997): MAT > 22 °C, MAP > 1200 mm, ≤ 6 months under 60 mm; or P > E in ≥ 6 months with ≥ 8
  months > 23 °C. **Ni laterite** (Thorne et al.): P > 1000 mm, cold month 15–27 °C, warm 22–31 °C, on ultramafics.
  Well-drained stable plateaus; saprolite 4–40 m/Myr, a mature profile 0.5–several Myr; uplift strips them, burial
  preserves.
- **Supergene copper** (Sillitoe 2005): ≥ 0.5 Myr, typically 3–9 Myr; uplift lowers the water table; hot semi-arid to
  pluvial, erosion slower than weathering; hyperaridity freezes it (Atacama 44–9 Ma).
- **Evaporites**: 15–35° arid belt (volume-weighted mean 23 ± 4°); cell test (Bao et al. 2023, pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/
  articles/PMC10171626): of months > 24 °C, ≥ 80% with P < 100 mm. Barred basins, sags, distal forelands, late syn-rift
  (South Atlantic Aptian salt behind the Walvis barrier, ~9 Myr, > 2 km).
- **Coal and peat** (Bao et al. 2023): a month is suitable if T > 10 °C and P > 40 mm; a cell qualifies if ≥ 80% of its
  months > 10 °C are; none if every month > 30 °C. Coal palaeolatitude: before 250 Ma 18°S–13°N; after, 36–58°N and
  23–58°S; the arid belt coal-poor. Subsidence 0.1–0.2 mm/yr for > 10⁵ yr; > 0.5 mm/yr drowns the mire. Forelands and
  rifts. Modern peat: post-glacial, growing-season warmth north of 30°.
- **Placers**: gold lags source exhumation by 20–70 Myr; mostly < 10–25 km from source; **ice strips placers** — the
  Klondike is rich because ice missed it (emrlibrary.gov.yk.ca/emrlibrary/ygs/yeg/2015/3_Bond.pdf); cassiterite half at
  the contact, rest ≤ 25 km; diamonds ~1000 km along the Namibian coast, richest within ~100 km of the Orange mouth;
  heavy-mineral sands at river mouths, headlands, fossil strandlines.
- **Bog iron**: acid iron-rich groundwater in wetlands; mostly sandy, formerly glaciated lowlands. **Oolitic ironstone**:
  shallow shelves, beds 2–5 m × 2–10 km, at high sea level (Ordovician–Devonian, Jurassic–Paleogene); Minette is
  phosphatic — usable only after 1879 (Industrial).
- **Fuel**: bloomery iron 10–18 kg charcoal per kg (63–150 kg wood); copper ~200–300 kg charcoal per kg (popular source).
- **Last glacial maximum**: −6.1 °C global, ~−14 °C Arctic; Ohmura's equilibrium line P = 645 + 296T + 9T² — already
  `finish-ice.js`.

## 5. Plumes, LIPs, kimberlites

- **LIPs**: > 0.1 Mkm², most volume in < 1–5 Myr; ~1 per 20 Myr long-term, ~1 per 10 Myr in the last 150 Myr; clustered.
  Every major continental flood basalt of the last 300 Myr goes with an ocean opening (lag < 10 to 20 Myr, *contested*);
  up to 90% of rifted margins volcanic (Bryan & Ferrari 2013). Deccan ~⅓ left after 66 Myr, Siberia ~10% of volume after
  252 Myr (*derived* time constant ~60–130 Myr); old LIPs survive as dyke swarms (radiating > 2000 km), sills and layered
  intrusions (Bushveld 350 × 250 km).
- **Plume generation zones** (Torsvik & Burke): LIPs, hotspots and ~80% of kimberlites erupt above the edges of two
  antipodal deep-mantle provinces (*contested*). Evolution uses a mantle-insulation rule instead.
- **Kimberlites**: diamondiferous only on Archean cratons with lithosphere ≥ 175 km (Clifford's rule); fields 30–50 km,
  5–50 pipes in clusters, 100–500 km apart; ~1% economic; mostly Mesozoic, peaks ~130 and ~90 Ma; ~20–30 Myr after
  breakup, migrating inland (Gernon 2023).
- **Associated**: Ni-Cu-PGE at craton margins, sulfur from evaporites (Noril'sk); Bushveld 90% of PGM reserves, 40–50%
  of chromium; Fe-Ti-V in big layered intrusions; native copper in rift flood basalts (Keweenaw); carbonatite Nb-REE in
  rifts; laterite and bauxite on basalt plateaus (Deccan).
- **Rifts**: alkaline volcanism, carbonatites, soda lakes (closed basins: only where the hydrology allows a salt sea —
  see the rivers-end-at-sea rule); failed arms become SEDEX hosts.

## 6. Oil and gas (added at the developer's request)

- **Source rocks** (Demaison & Moore 1980): anoxic bottom water — warm stratified lakes, silled basins with positive
  water balance (Black Sea), upwelling margins, ocean anoxic events; preservation over productivity. Lacustrine rift
  sources: ~90% of China's, ~95% of Brazil's reserves (pre-salt). Kerogen I (lakes) and II (marine) → oil; III (land
  plants, coal) → gas (Groningen: Carboniferous coal under Zechstein salt).
- **Age clustering** (Klemme & Ulmishek 1991, searchanddiscovery.com/documents/Animator/klemme2.htm): six intervals hold
  > 90% — Upper Jurassic 25%, mid-Cretaceous 29%, Oligocene–Miocene 12.5%, Silurian 9%, Devonian 8%, Pennsylvanian–Permian
  8%; mostly transgressive (high sea level, greenhouse, OAEs); low palaeolatitude → oil-prone, high → gas and coal; the
  Tethys < ⅕ of the area, > ⅔ of reserves. 70% generated in the last ~89 Myr.
- **Maturation**: oil ~60–150 °C, gas ~150–200 °C, spent > ~220 °C; gradients 45 °C/km (young rifts, back-arcs), ~25–30
  average, 20–25 (forelands, old cratons). Lopatin TTI = Σ Δt·2^((T−105)/10): oil 15–160. Foreland loading the main
  trigger. Uplift stops generation; < 80 °C biodegrades → heavy oil, tar sands (Athabasca, Orinoco) on the craton side of
  forelands (USGS OFR 2007-1084).
- **Traps and seals**: giants (≥ 500 MMboe, ~932, ~40% of reserves) — passive margins 34.7%, rifts and sags 30.9%,
  collisions 19.7% (Mann et al. 2003; *contested* — other schemes put > 80% in forelands); Zagros ~7% of world reserves.
  Evaporites < 2% of sediment, seal ~half the largest fields; carbonate reservoirs ~50–60%. Arabia: Jurassic source,
  carbonate reservoir, anhydrite seal, later Zagros folding. Field sizes Pareto (~20% of fields hold 80%).
- **Preservation** (Macgregor 1996): giant fields' median age 35 Myr; best under salt in quiet basins.
- **Pre-industrial use**: bitumen hafting ~70–40 ka (Umm el Tlel); caulked boats 5500–5000 BCE; Mesopotamian mortar;
  Dead Sea bitumen to Egypt; mummies from ~1550 BCE; Greek fire ~672; Baku hand-dug pits (9th c.); Sichuan drilled gas
  wells by AD 347 (brine boiling); Pitch Lake. Industrial: Pechelbronn 1745, Baku 1846, Drake 1859. Offshore only modern
  (~30% of production now). (Chimaera's flames are not petroleum.)

## 7. Precedents and presentation

- **Games**: Dwarf Fortress (ore tied to host rock, site summaries; layers without history); TerraFirmaCraft (ore by host
  rock, poor/normal/rich, copper → bronze → iron); Vintage Story (USGS-style provinces from noise; poor → bountiful);
  Azgaar (mines by height, fixed weights — tin as common as copper); Civ (balanced for fairness); Victoria 3 (capped,
  discoverable resources). Gleba derives rocks from tectonics but no ores. **No tool places ores from a simulated
  history.**
- **What worldbuilders want**: scarcity (tin), "where could a Bronze Age start", per-region summaries, distance to the
  scarce metal (trade), a reason to quote. Complaints: even scatter, all deposits equal, ores unrelated to mountains,
  icon clutter, everything everywhere by the Industrial age, no export.
- **Maps**: commodity by colour, deposit type by shape, size by three symbol sizes (GSJ / Circum-Pacific); USGS ore belts
  as polygons per time slice (pubs.usgs.gov/publication/sim3022). On a globe and a phone, filled areas beat symbols; ≤ 8–9
  hues; grade by opacity, never hue.
- **Exports**: equirect PNGs, transparent overlays, 8-bit masks per commodity, GeoJSON/CSV of deposits (Azgaar's GIS
  export is the precedent).
- **Stories**: Dwarf Fortress Legends, Caves of Qud's history grammars; here each deposit's origin and later fate come
  from the real history (*"formed 140 Myr ago behind an island arc; scraped onto the continent when the arc docked 60
  Myr ago; bared by erosion since"*).
