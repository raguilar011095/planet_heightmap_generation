A planet's history

Evolution simulates a planet's tectonic history: a supercontinent breaks up, oceans open and close, and the continents collide and gather again.

A history is already building behind this card — you can watch it while you read.

Evolution works best on a desktop computer for now. It runs on a phone with lighter settings: coarser plates, and Add detail up to 640K cells. Its Food tab is new and in beta, and neither it nor Geology has been tried on a phone yet.

Scrub the timeline

Every moment on the timeline is a Preview: a quick look at the planet at that time. Drag the timeline to any moment, or press play. The shaded part of the rail is the history computed so far, and you can scrub through all of it while the rest fills in.

Drag the globe to turn it, scroll to zoom, and hover the planet for its height, crust and climate.

Add detail

Found a moment you like? Press Add detail. It sculpts that moment again, much more finely: rivers and lakes, glaciers and fjords, sand seas, its own climate, and the Satellite, Geology and Food views. It takes from a few seconds to about a minute.

One moment is kept in detail at a time. Adding detail to another replaces it once the new one is done, and the card tells you when it will.

Look around

Switch between Terrain, Satellite, Climate, Geology, Food, Heightmap and Plates with the tabs over the planet. Clouds drift over the Satellite view, from the planet’s own climate; turn them off with Clouds beside Hillshade.

Climate shows Köppen zones, temperature, rain, wind and currents, with more layers (pressure, upwelling, ice, aridity, growing season and others) under More… or in the layer list. It also works on a paused preview.

The arrow at the tabs' left opens the view options: Globe or Map, the map in any of ten projections (scroll to zoom it, drag to move around), the terrain's colours, and Hillshade, which turns the relief's shading off for flat colour.

Geology

On a detailed moment, Geology shows what the history left in the rock.

Rocks — what the land is made of. Hover a rock for how it came to be there.

Deposits — metals, fuels and minerals, poor to rich: all together, one metal at a time, or by the kind of deposit.

Ages — what the Stone, Copper, Bronze, Iron and Industrial Ages could reach, and fuel, gems and building stone. Tap a legend entry to see it alone, and hover a deposit for its district, its rank on the world and how it formed.

Food Beta

On a detailed moment, Food shows what the land could feed.

Farmland — green where rain alone grows crops, aqua where only water brought from rivers makes it farmable. Switch the farming techniques on and off in its legend.

Staple food — what each place would live on most: paddy grains, cool- or warm-season grains, roots, livestock, fish or wild foods. Lifestyle — whether its people would farm, herd or gather.

People — how many the land could feed. Soil and Growing season show what lies behind it.

Export

Export saves the detailed moment as map files: a colour map, the Satellite view (the year, January or July, with or without its clouds), the clouds alone, its climate zones, heightmaps with and without the sea floor, a land mask, and its rivers and lakes on their own. Every climate and food view can go too, each as colour or as Data — 16-bit greyscales on fixed scales.

From Geology: its rocks, its deposits and each age's map, a richness mask for every resource, the districts as GeoJSON or CSV, and a resource atlas in words.

Several maps save as one ZIP with a README, at any width up to 16384 px, in any of ten map projections, centred as you like, with or without the hillshade.

Keep a planet

Found a world worth keeping? In Export, Save planet puts its whole history and its detailed moment in one file. Load planet… in World opens it again later, or drop the file anywhere on the page — with no waiting for either.

The file holds the history itself, so it opens exactly as it was saved, in any browser. Extend history on an opened planet first runs the history again here from its seed (as long as it took to build), and then goes on from where the file ends — if it comes out the same in this browser.

Steer it

New world starts a fresh history from a random seed, one of over two billion. Open World to set the seed (any number up to 2,147,483,645, or any word or phrase: the same word always builds the same world), the starting continent size, how long the history runs, how it should end, the planet's Temperature and Precipitation, and the Quality.

At the end of a history, Extend history runs it on for as long as you like. The link in your address bar rebuilds the same history, so you can share it.
