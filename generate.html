World Orogen — Procedural Planet & World Map Generator

World Orogen is a free, browser-based procedural planet generator: believable worlds from the ground up, in a studio of planet tools that gets you from a blank page to a world that makes sense — no download or account required. Its planets are shaped by tectonic plates, erosion, volcanism and a seasonal climate, with the science behind each layer simplified but real. Three tools share one climate: Generate builds a planet from sliders, Import runs the climate on your own map, and Evolution simulates a planet's whole tectonic history, with the rocks, ores and food its land could offer.

How It Works

Start by adjusting sliders for detail level, number of tectonic plates, continents, and terrain roughness. Click "Build new world" to generate a unique planet with realistic continental shapes, mountain ranges, ocean trenches, volcanic island chains, and hotspot volcanism. Every planet is procedurally generated and completely unique.

Key Features

  • Tectonic plate simulation with convergent, divergent, and transform boundaries
  • Every planet finished with a past: rivers carved by its climate's runoff, lakes, a last ice age, dunes and loess, a rock map, ore, fuel and gem deposits, and what its land could feed
  • Climate simulation with seasonal wind patterns, ocean currents, precipitation, and Köppen climate classification
  • Hotspot volcanism with drift-trail island chains (like Hawaii)
  • Interactive plate editing — select multiple plates for batch land/ocean toggling with visual preview before rebuild
  • Your own map's climate — bring an equirectangular B&W heightmap (of a map you drew, of Earth or Mars, or from another tool) and see its winds, rain, temperatures and Köppen climate zones, its rivers and lakes, a satellite view with clouds and what its land could grow
  • Evolution — watch a planet's continents rift, drift and collide over a billion years, then sculpt any moment in detail with rivers, glaciers, its own climate, a satellite view and a geology view: a rock-type map, ore, fuel, mineral and gem deposits built from that history — each district with the story of how it formed — what each technological age could mine, and exports of them all (maps, richness masks, GeoJSON, a resource atlas) — and a food view (what its land could grow, what each place would live on most, and whether its people would farm, herd or gather); and save the planet as a file to open again later
  • Temperature and precipitation controls that work through the climate: cool a planet and its ice spreads, warm it and it grows wetter
  • Multiple visualization modes: terrain with rivers and lakes, a satellite view with clouds in summer and winter, climate, geology, food, heightmap and plates
  • Detailed layers for the climate and the world's past — pressure and wind, ocean currents, upwelling, sea ice, temperature, precipitation, aridity, growing season and more, for the year, January or July
  • Map export up to 65,536px wide (above 16,384px on a computer, equirectangular, whole or in 16,384px tiles): every map as colour or as 16-bit data on documented scales (heightmaps, hillshade, temperature, rain, winds, currents, ice, Köppen classes, rocks, deposits, food), in ten map projections, one PNG or a ZIP with a README
  • Map making — a believable base for a fantasy or world map: the terrain in sixteen colour palettes (Relief, Topographic, Swiss, School atlas, Antique and more), satellite maps with or without clouds, Köppen climate maps, and rivers and lakes, clouds, a land mask and hillshade as separate layers, in any of ten projections
  • Shareable planet codes — copy a code to share your exact planet with others

Use Cases

World Orogen is used by worldbuilders, game developers, tabletop RPG players, fantasy authors, and anyone who needs realistic terrain. Export heightmaps for use in game engines like Unity or Unreal, or download satellite-style maps for your D&D campaign setting. The climate simulation produces realistic biome distributions for believable fantasy worlds.

Mapmakers use it as the ground under a fantasy map: export the terrain, rivers, climate zones and hillshade, then trace, paint over or bring them into a map-making tool. Every world, from its plates to its climate, is procedurally generated, not AI-generated, and the maps are yours to use, commercially too.

Share Your World

Every planet has a unique planet code. Share the URL (e.g. orogen.studio/generate#CODE) to let anyone recreate your exact world — including any tectonic plates you've edited by hand.

Welcome to World Orogen

Procedural planets inspired by real tectonics, erosion, and climate.

Every world is one of a kind — with believable continents, mountains, ocean trenches, and volcanic islands. Build one in seconds, tweak it until it's yours.

Shape your world

Use the Shape your world sliders to control detail, plates, continents, and roughness, then hit Build new world. The Detail slider only changes rendering resolution — continent shapes stay the same, so you can iterate quickly at low detail and crank it up when the planet looks good. Expand Terrain sculpting to fine-tune erosion and smoothing — these can be reapplied instantly with the ↻ button.

The planet shows in seconds; a few seconds later its climate, rivers and lakes, ice age, rocks and food are worked out in the background, and the Satellite, Climate, Geology and Food views open.

Explore & edit

Drag to rotate the globe. Scroll to zoom in and out. Ctrl-click plates (⌘-click on a Mac) to mark them for reshaping — select multiple, then hit Rebuild to apply all at once. Ctrl-click again to undo a pending selection. Hover any place to see its plate and crust, height, river or lake, climate — and on the Geology and Food views its rock, ores and what the land could feed.

Visualize your world

Switch between Terrain, Satellite, Climate, Geology, Food, Heightmap and Plates with the tabs over the planet. Climate opens its own row of maps — Köppen, temperature, rain, wind, currents, and under More… pressure, sea ice, aridity, growing season and more — each for the year, January or July (the Satellite view too); Geology its rocks, deposits and what each technological age could reach; Food its farmland, staple foods, ways of life and people. The arrow at the tabs' left opens the view options above them: Globe or Map, the map in any of ten projections, its centre, the grid, the terrain's Colours, the hillshade, the clouds and the rivers; the world's past is in Inspect (advanced) in the sidebar.

Save & share

Every planet gets a unique planet code shown below the Build button — including any plates you've edited. Copy it to share with others, or paste a code and click Load to recreate someone else's world. You can also share the URL directly.

Use Export map to save any map the page draws — the terrain, satellite (with clouds or without), climate zones, every climate layer, rivers and lakes, heightmaps, a hillshade, plates, rocks, deposits, food — as Colour (as shown) or as Data (16-bit greyscales on fixed scales, for other tools), for the year, January or July, in ten map projections, centred where you like. One map saves as a PNG; several as a ZIP with a README that explains every file.

What's new in World Orogen 3.0

Every page has a new look, in Paper or Night, the Studio included. Generate is rebuilt on Evolution's ground: its worlds now have a past, with plates that move and mountains worn to their age, and they get Evolution's Geology and Food tabs. In Evolution, ridges are more varied, deposits are coloured by metal family, and Geology is out of beta.

Heads up: A saved Generate planet code builds the same continents in the same places, with new coasts, mountains and rivers. An Evolution link gives the same history, its ridges drawn a little differently. To see either exactly as it was, open it at orogen.studio/v/2.3/generate or orogen.studio/v/2.3/evolution.

A new look

Every page is redrawn, calmer, so the planet is the star.

  • Paper and Night — A light and a dark theme. They follow your device; the sun-and-moon switch in the top bar overrides it, and every page remembers your choice.
  • The Studio — The home page turns a planet through its views, and shows each tool with two worlds made in it.
  • A calmer globe — Lit from the upper left wherever you turn it, with a thin rim of air, on the page's own colour. Stars are still an option.
  • The degree ring — A gold ring with its degrees around the globe, and a frame with degrees around every map, in every projection.
  • Loading that says what's happening — In Evolution, each stage of Add detail is named as it runs.

Generate, rebuilt

Generate now builds its worlds on Evolution's ground, and still takes seconds.

  • Plates that move — The pieces you edit are grouped into plates that carry continents and ocean together. Most coasts are passive, and each continent has an active margin.
  • A past — Continents are assembled from their pieces, with old mountain belts along their seams, cratons, hotspot chains and flood basalts. Coasts on either side of an ocean fit together.
  • Mountains with a cause — Andean margins, collisions, island arcs and rift valleys, carved into massifs, each worn down to its age.
  • Geology and Food — Evolution's Geology and Food (beta) tabs, with their exports and readout.
  • Editing — Ctrl-click a plate and Rebuild: the edit changes the place you edit and little else. And in every tool, the poles no longer smear.

Also in 3.0

  • Varied ridges in Evolution — Mountain ridges bend, rise and fall at every scale, with peaks and saddles along each one, and old belts are a little less even. The history itself is the same.
  • Deposits by metal family — Four colours instead of eleven: iron and alloy, base, precious and salt, each with its row in the legend.
  • Geology, out of beta — Its rocks, deposits and ages. Food stays in beta.
  • Import — Your map gets the new Satellite view, with clouds, and Food (beta). Import Heightmap is now just Import.
  • Phones, tablets and keyboards — Touch targets of 44 px on phones, plate editing on tablets, and dialogs that hold the keyboard's focus.

Since 2.0

  • Evolution — A planet's whole tectonic history, over up to one and a half billion years: play it like a film, share its link, or save it as a planet file and open it again later.
  • Add detail — Any moment sculpted again, finely: rivers through the seasons, glaciers and fjords, sand seas, and a climate of its own, with its clouds.
  • Export anything — Every map on every page, as colour or as 16-bit data, in ten map projections.
  • Older versions online — Each version since 2.1 stays at orogen.studio/v/<version>/, so links and planet files open as they were made.
