World Orogen — Procedural Planet Generator

World Orogen is a free, browser-based procedural planet generator: believable worlds from the ground up, in a studio of planet tools that gets you from a blank page to a world that makes sense — no download or account required. Its planets are shaped by tectonic plates, erosion, volcanism and a seasonal climate, with the science behind each layer simplified but real. Three tools share one climate: Generate builds a planet from sliders, Import Heightmap runs the climate on your own map, and Evolution simulates a planet's whole tectonic history, with the rocks, ores and food its land could offer.

How It Works

Start by adjusting sliders for detail level, number of tectonic plates, continents, and terrain roughness. Click "Build New World" to generate a unique planet with realistic continental shapes, mountain ranges, ocean trenches, volcanic island chains, and hotspot volcanism. Every planet is procedurally generated and completely unique.

Key Features

  • Tectonic plate simulation with convergent, divergent, and transform boundaries
  • Multiple erosion types: glacial (fjords, U-shaped valleys), hydraulic (river valleys), and thermal (talus slopes)
  • Climate simulation with seasonal wind patterns, ocean currents, precipitation, and Köppen climate classification
  • Hotspot volcanism with drift-trail island chains (like Hawaii)
  • Interactive plate editing — select multiple plates for batch land/ocean toggling with visual preview before rebuild
  • Heightmap import — bring your own equirectangular B&W heightmap (Earth, Mars, hand-drawn) and run full climate simulation on it
  • Evolution — watch a planet's continents rift, drift and collide over a billion years, then sculpt any moment in detail with rivers, glaciers, its own climate, a satellite view and a geology view: a rock-type map, ore, fuel, mineral and gem deposits built from that history — each district with the story of how it formed — what each technological age could mine, and exports of them all (maps, richness masks, GeoJSON, a resource atlas) — and a food view (what its land could grow, what each place would live on most, and whether its people would farm, herd or gather); and save the planet as a file to open again later
  • Temperature and precipitation controls that work through the climate: cool a planet and its ice spreads, warm it and it grows wetter
  • Multiple visualization modes: terrain, a satellite view in summer and winter, climate, and heightmap
  • Detailed inspection layers for geology and climate — pressure and wind, ocean currents, upwelling, sea ice, temperature, precipitation, aridity, growing season and more, for the year, January or July
  • Map export up to 65,536px wide: every map as colour or as 16-bit data on documented scales (heightmaps, hillshade, temperature, rain, winds, currents, ice, Köppen classes), in ten map projections, one PNG or a ZIP with a README
  • Shareable planet codes — copy a code to share your exact planet with others

Use Cases

World Orogen is used by worldbuilders, game developers, tabletop RPG players, fantasy authors, and anyone who needs realistic terrain. Export heightmaps for use in game engines like Unity or Unreal, or download satellite-style maps for your D&D campaign setting. The climate simulation produces realistic biome distributions for believable fantasy worlds.

Share Your World

Every planet has a unique planet code. Share the URL (e.g. orogen.studio/generate#CODE) to let anyone recreate your exact world — including any tectonic plates you've edited by hand.

Welcome to World Orogen

Procedural planets inspired by real tectonics, erosion, and climate.

Every world is one of a kind — with believable continents, mountains, ocean trenches, and volcanic islands. Build one in seconds, tweak it until it's yours.

Shape Your World

Use the Shape Your World sliders to control detail, plates, continents, and roughness, then hit Build New World. The Detail slider only changes rendering resolution — continent shapes stay the same, so you can iterate quickly at low detail and crank it up when the planet looks good. Expand Terrain Sculpting to fine-tune erosion and smoothing — these can be reapplied instantly with the ↻ button.

Explore & Edit

Drag to rotate the globe. Scroll to zoom in and out. Ctrl-click plates (⌘-click on a Mac) to mark them for reshaping — select multiple, then hit Rebuild to apply all at once. Ctrl-click again to undo a pending selection. Hover any region to see elevation, coordinates, temperature, precipitation, and climate classification.

Visualize Your World

Switch between Terrain, Satellite, Climate, and Heightmap views, and Plates, with the tabs over the planet. Climate opens its own row of maps — Köppen, temperature, rain, wind, currents, and under More… pressure, sea ice, aridity, growing season and more — each for the year, January or July (the Satellite view too). The arrow at the tabs' left opens the view options above them: Globe or Map, the map in any of ten projections, its centre, the grid and the terrain's Colours — Classic or one of Evolution's map styles and other-world looks; the terrain's inner layers are in Inspect (advanced) in the sidebar.

The climate takes a few seconds at any detail. On phones, at the very highest detail it is computed when you first switch to a climate view.

Save & Share

Every planet gets a unique planet code shown below the Build button — including any plates you've edited. Copy it to share with others, or paste a code and click Load to recreate someone else's world. You can also share the URL directly.

Use Export Map to save any map the page draws — the terrain, satellite, climate zones, every climate layer, heightmaps, a hillshade, plates — as Colour (as shown) or as Data (16-bit greyscales on fixed scales, for other tools), for the year, January or July, in ten map projections, centred where you like. One map saves as a PNG; several as a ZIP with a README that explains every file.

What's New in World Orogen

In Evolution, the Satellite view is redrawn, and clouds worked out from each planet's own climate now drift over it. In Geology, the deposits take their shapes from the rock: districts along mountain belts and fault lines, each metal in its own provinces, and coal, oil and gas at every latitude.

Heads up: A saved Evolution link or seed grows the same continents as before, but its deposits are placed anew. Planet files keep the deposits they were saved with. To see a link exactly as it was, open it at orogen.studio/v/2.2/evolution. Generate and Import are unchanged.

The Satellite View, Redrawn

On a detailed moment, the Satellite view now reads more like a planet seen from space, from the whole globe down to a single valley.

  • The land in the colour — Valleys and basins richer, ridges paler, highlands greyer, and the tree line and snow line bending with the ground. Each climate's base colour, by its warmth and rain, is fitted to Earth's as seen from space.
  • Climates that blend — Neighbouring climates fade into each other instead of meeting at a line, with a faint patchwork on top. Rock and snow still follow the height.
  • The ground's character — Bedrock tints, sand seas streaked by the wind, banded fold belts, patchy forest canopy, the margins of the ice sheets, and broad floodplains, basins and plateaus.
  • Green rivers — Rivers and fresh lakes green the dry country along them, in the place's own colours.
  • A smoother globe — Zoomed out, every view averages its detail instead of speckling.

Clouds from the Climate

Clouds now drift over the Satellite view, worked out from the detailed moment's own climate: more where it rains, fewer under the dry highs. Turn them off with Clouds, beside Hillshade.

  • Four layers — Low decks over cool seas and the polar oceans, towering clouds in the wet tropics and over warm summer land, sheets along the storm tracks, and high cirrus streaked along the wind.
  • Storms — Spirals at the season's pressure lows and along the storm tracks, and tropical cyclones over seas of 27 °C or more.
  • Always moving — They drift with the planet's winds and change shape as they go. The year, January and July each have their own.
  • Export — Two new maps: the clouds alone, on a transparent background, and Satellite with clouds.

Deposits Shaped by the Rock Beta

In the Geology tab, deposits now take their shapes from the geology, and noise only breaks a shape into districts.

  • Districts like real ones — Clusters of many sizes, drawn out along mountain belts and ancient shields and gathered on fault lines, with lobed outlines. Each metal has its own provinces, so different metals no longer sit in the same clumps.
  • Layers that crop out — Coal, ironstone and oil shale appear where the land cuts through their layer, as on the Rocks map. Laterite and bauxite cap old uplands, peat and bog iron lie on wet ground, and salt flats in dry pans.
  • Oil, gas and coal — Source rock at any latitude and on continental shelves, coal on the coastal plains behind shallow seas, and oil gathered under fold crests and arches.
  • Iron and stone — Iron in most mountain belts, banded iron only in greenstone belts and at craton edges. Building stone where there's rock to quarry and it's easy to get at (basalt and tuff now count), clay where stone is scarce, and stone for knapping almost anywhere off the great plains.
